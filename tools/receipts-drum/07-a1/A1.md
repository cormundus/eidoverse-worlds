# A1 — did two people keep playing? (2026-10-06)

**Verdict: yes, by ear.** One human (Cormundus, live in Chrome on the hitter's pad) and one
scripted resident (the real `WorldAgent.play`, the same path as the MCPL `play` tool), on an
owned scratch world, at 90 BPM in 4/4 sixteenths. The resident kept a groove on the low drum,
pre-submitting four bars at a time; the human played the hand drum live.

In the player's words:

> "feels solid, feels like it's in time, feels like I'm having a good play, getting into the groove"

The design asks for 32 bars with the human at least partly live. Both demo runs went far
past that: the first ran 20 minutes (444 bars), the second 25.

## The record

- **The first demo world** (kit v1, retuned live to v2 at about minute 8): the resident had
  **113 passages accepted, 0 refused** over 20 minutes, including straight through the retune.
  The kit change bumped both drums' `voiceGen`, and the resident's next passage carried the new
  one without any handling on its part. The steps it had already queued at the old tuning were
  voided, not played in the wrong voice, which Adam heard as a short gap.
- **The second demo world** (kit v2 from the start, every pad fix in): **141 passages accepted,
  0 refused** over 25 minutes (`demo-2.txt`; the first is `demo-1.txt`). At the end, the
  resident's own `look()` shows the human's playing exactly as a text-tier body gets it: grid
  positions and stroke letters (`hand (adam): ..T..T..........  ......T.......T.  …`), with no
  timing slop and no loudness.
- **That same look() exposed a perception bug:** the human's bars were from about bar 42 and the
  resident's from bar 564, under one span line ("bars 42–564"), with nothing saying the hand drum
  had gone quiet some 20 minutes earlier. True, and misleading. Fixed after this record (each
  drum line now carries its own bars, and how long it has been quiet).
- Teardown on both: the child exited and the port closed.
